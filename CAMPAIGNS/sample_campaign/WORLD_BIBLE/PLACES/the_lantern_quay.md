# The Lantern Quay

**The Lantern Quay** curves along the Lower Quay seawall three piers inward from the Outer Boom. Named for the row of iron-caged whale-oil lamps nailed to its timber awnings, it serves night-shift chainmen, barge hands, and dockside traders who work between tides.

## Notable Landmarks

- **Elena Reed's Apothecary**: A narrow brick-and-shingle storefront wedged between a sail-mender's loft and a salted-herring cellar. Shelves hold amber glass bottles, dried sea-fennel, pine pitch, and brass mortar bowls.
- **The Salt & Anchor Taproom**: A low-ceilinged dock tavern smelling of boiled cabbage, wet wool, and cheap rye ale. Night-shift ferrymen and collier deckhands trade gossip here before dawn muster.
- **The Ferry Steps**: Slick limestone stairs descending straight into the tidal basin, where skiffmen pole passengers across to the Outer Wharf for two Copper Pence.

## Sensory Anchors

- **Sounds**: Iron tavern signs creaking in the salt wind, clatter of wooden clogs on wet cobblestones, and muffled voices behind shuttered windows.
- **Smells**: Pine pitch, fried fish oil, damp mortar, and vinegar-washed timber.
